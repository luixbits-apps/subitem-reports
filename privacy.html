Privacy Policy — Subitem Reports

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Subitem Reports is a dashboard widget for monday.com. It is developed and operated by Luiz Perren, an independent developer based in Switzerland (Oberschönenbuch 31, 6438 Ibach, Switzerland; "I", "me" in this policy). Write to contact@luizperren.dev with any privacy question or request. monday.com is a trademark of monday.com Ltd.; Subitem Reports is not affiliated with or endorsed by monday.com Ltd.

In short

  • The widget runs in your browser, inside your monday.com dashboard. It reads the boards you choose through monday's API, read-only, with the permissions of the person viewing the dashboard.
  • Everything it saves stays inside your monday account, in the app storage that monday provides for your account: each widget's report configuration, and the trend history, "What changed" records and usage counters.
  • I run no server that the widget talks to. The widget sends requests to monday.com and nowhere else, so no board data, e-mail address, name, token or identifier reaches me or any other company through it. I keep no copy of what it saves.
  • The only personal data I hold is what you send me when you e-mail support.
  • If you use the optional "Describe a report" action, it sends the request you typed plus board names and column titles to monday's AI gateway, only when you use it, on your account's monday AI credits (section 6).

3. What the widget reads and where it runs

Where it runs. The widget is a set of static files hosted by monday.com on its app CDN and loaded into an iframe on your dashboard. All of its code runs in your browser. None of it runs on a server of mine.

What it reads. The widget calls monday's GraphQL API through the monday SDK with the session of the user who is viewing the dashboard, so it can only read what that user can already see. It requests the boards:read permission. For the optional AI action in section 6 it also requests ai:consume. For the boards selected in a report it reads:

  • board names, groups and columns, including each column's title, type, status labels and label colors;
  • items and subitems at every level: names and the column values the report needs (status, people, dates, numbers, text and similar);
  • item and subitem counts; for the Workload view, people assignments, dates and hour values.

It also reads from monday: your account id and user id, whether you are an admin or a view-only user, whether the dashboard is in edit mode, the dashboard's theme, the boards connected to the dashboard, and your account's subscription to the app (plan, trial, days left) through monday's app_subscription API.

What it tells monday. When a report first renders and when you export, the widget sends monday's "value created" event, which monday uses for its marketplace statistics and which carries no board data. When you click the plan or rating buttons it asks monday to open monday's own plan picker or rating dialog.

Nothing is uploaded. Board data is held in your browser's memory while the dashboard is open and discarded when the widget closes. Exports (CSV, PNG, copy as image, PDF report) are generated in your browser and saved or copied where you choose. They are not sent anywhere by the app.

Read-only. The app has no write permission on boards. It cannot create, change, move or delete anything in your boards.

5. What is never stored or sent

  • No servers of mine. The widget makes no request to any server operated by me. A hosting slot on monday's app hosting is reserved for a future version; in this version it answers only an automated health check, is never called by the widget and holds no data.
  • No e-mail addresses, names or tokens from the widget. The widget never sends me your users' e-mail addresses or names (section 7 covers what monday's Developer Center shows me). The widget uses the monday session already in your browser; I never see or store a monday access token or API key.
  • No copies of board data. Nothing from your boards is stored outside monday. Inside your account the app stores only what section 4 lists: each widget's report configuration, one number per day per report metric, the What changed records and the usage counters.
  • No analytics, tracking, crash reporting or third-party scripts. The widget loads from monday's CDN, embeds no fonts or scripts from other domains, sets no cookies and sends no request to any address outside monday. A logo for the PDF report is a file you upload; it is stored in the report settings and never fetched from anywhere.
  • No logs. The widget sends no logs to me. Messages it writes to the browser console stay in your browser.
  • No e-mail from the app. The app sends no e-mail to anyone, and offers no e-mail delivery of reports.

6. AI

The widget offers one optional AI action, "Describe a report".

Only when you ask. AI runs only when you type a request into "Describe a report…" on the Chart and submit it, or click one of its example chips. Nothing is sent otherwise. The chart, tables, exports and trend never use AI.

What is sent. Your request (up to 200 characters), the names of the selected boards, the titles and types of their columns (each under a short alias, not monday's column id), and the item and subitem counts. Not sent: cell values, item names, people names, status or group labels, or totals. Board names and column titles are text your account chose and can contain personal or confidential words, so I do not describe this data as anonymous. The "What is sent" link under the input repeats this.

Where it goes. To monday's AI gateway (monday's run_prompt API), on your account's monday AI credits, and only if an admin of your account has granted the app's AI gateway permission. monday forwards the request to its AI model providers under monday's AI terms and monday's sub-processor list. I have no account or key with any AI provider and cannot see the request or the answer.

What comes back. Only report settings chosen from your board's actual columns (what to group by, what to measure, which chart). The widget's own code computes every number. AI can misread a request, so check the settings it applied; the widget marks them "Set up with AI" and offers Undo.

9. Cookies and browser storage

The widget sets no cookies and contains no tracking. It uses your browser's local storage, inside the widget's own iframe, for per-viewer conveniences only: the view you last used, whether the report editor is collapsed, the dashboard theme reported last time (so the widget paints in the right colors before monday reports the theme), whether you dismissed the one-time next-steps strip and the trial reminders, which trial notes you have seen, that you accepted the Terms and Privacy notice, and when you were last offered the rating dialog (the last two stored under your monday user id, so each user on a shared computer is asked only once). These values never leave your browser, are not sent to me or to monday, and disappear when you clear site data. If your browser blocks local storage the widget still works with defaults.

These pages on luixbits-apps.github.io set no cookies, run no analytics and load no resources from other domains. GitHub, which hosts them, receives the usual server-log data for each request (IP address, page requested, time, browser type) in order to deliver the page; I do not use it to identify or track you. GDPR legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f).

How to exercise them:

12. Deleting data

  • One report's history. In the dashboard's edit mode, open the Trend view and choose "Delete this history". This removes that report's recorded series, its index entry and its What changed record, for everyone in the account.
  • All history the app stored for your account. An account admin, in the dashboard's edit mode, opens the widget's Help menu and chooses "Delete all Subitem Reports history". This removes every trend series, What changed record and usage counter the app saved in your account's app storage. Report configurations are not part of the history and stay as they are.
  • Report configuration. Change it in the widget's Edit report. Removing the widget stops its configuration from being read but does not delete it; it is deleted with the rest of the app's storage for your account on request (below). When the widget cannot read its saved configuration, it offers "Reset report", which deletes that widget's configuration.
  • Uninstalling the app removes the widget from every dashboard in your account. I do not claim that uninstalling automatically erases app storage. You can delete the history in the widget before you uninstall; to be certain that everything is gone, report configurations included, ask me as described next.
  • On request. An admin of your monday account can e-mail contact@luizperren.dev with the account id. After I have verified that the request comes from an admin of that account, I run monday's developer command mapps storage:remove-data -a 12150920 -c <accountId>, which removes all of the app's storage for that account (every widget's report configuration, every trend series, What changed record and usage counter), and confirm by e-mail within 10 days. This command removes everything the app stored for the account; it cannot remove one report's history selectively.
  • Support correspondence. E-mail me and I delete the thread, unless an open legal claim requires me to keep it.
  • Browser storage. Clear your browser's site data for monday.com.

13. Security and incidents

All traffic between the widget and monday.com uses HTTPS, provided by monday. The widget is a static bundle that contains no secrets and holds no tokens; reading boards and writing app storage both require the monday session of a logged-in user of your account, which monday checks. The public pages are served over HTTPS with HSTS. I keep a record of the deletion requests I carry out.

If I become aware of a security incident involving the app, I notify monday.com within 24 hours and before notifying users, as monday's Developer Terms require, then notify affected accounts without undue delay through monday.com and these pages. For personal data I control (support correspondence), I notify the FDPIC as soon as possible where a breach is likely to result in a high risk (Art. 24 FADP) and the competent EU supervisory authority within 72 hours where the GDPR requires it (Art. 33).
